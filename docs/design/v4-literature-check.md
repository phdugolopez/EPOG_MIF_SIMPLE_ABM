Each mechanism and each variant is compared with the texts
in Zotero (library `perso` unless noted). The page references come from the PDFs read in the
morning; no new text was read for this pass. Nothing in the model or the proposal was changed.

Verdicts: **compatible** (the rule is in the source), **simplification** (a known rule, cut
down), **departure** (the source says something else), **no source** (a device of the model).

## At a glance

- **The new defaults bring the model closer to Shaikh.** 16 % is the normal rate of his
  tables, cost-edge cutting is his price leadership, and entry that brings the price down to
  the price of production (prix de production) is his account of competition between
  industries.
- **Four points of the morning are settled**: the general rate, the starting price above the
  price of production, the missing way down for the price, and the rules agreeing on the
  starting menu.
- **Two results now go against Shaikh and should be reported as such.** The two labels
  realise the same profit rate (H6). And the advantage falls to drift when the profit-rate
  rule judges at the price of production (H5), the very prices at which he argues.
- **H5 has no mechanism yet.** The hurdle hardly moves between λ = 0 and λ = 1, so the fall
  of the advantage is not explained by the choice of technique as the literature has it.
- **The price wars go further than the text.** They run down to the rivals' unit cost; in
  Shaikh the cost leader need not go that far. Nothing in the sources read gives the size of
  the turbulence of the mixed population.
- **Still open from the morning**: the name "leader-follow", the E0 line on the industry
  profit rate, E0 without technical change, the vocabulary of "operating cost", and the two
  devices without a source (reserve condition, idle scrapping).
- **New devices without a source**: the scaled menu away from 16 % and the weight of market
  share in the entrant's label, through which 0.16 of the market moves.

- **Vocabulary.** Shaikh's "operating cost" includes depreciation (Table 7.1, p. 263; Table
  7.15, p. 319); his prime cost is ucs (p. 316). The model's "operating cost" is his prime
  cost. Say it once in the report.
- **The turbulence.** A price at 1.9 times the price of production, capacity between 0.29
  and 1.49 times demand and 7 % of demand lost are beyond anything the sources describe.
  Shaikh's fat and lean years (pp. 268–269) give no size, and Hall & Hitch 1939 report
  stable prices. The report should present these figures as the model's, not as a fact.
